# Editorial and technical style

## Voice

Be direct, independent, and useful. Explain what a mechanism does before why it matters. Avoid hype, unexplained acronyms, and claims such as “secure,” “official,” or “verified” without evidence.

Write in complete, natural sentences, the way you would explain the idea to a colleague. Direct does not mean clipped: keep the connecting words (so, because, instead of), and do not drop articles or compress clauses to save space.

## Technical claims

- Use the current official MCP specification as the primary protocol source.
- State the applicable specification version.
- Distinguish normative requirements, common patterns, and author recommendations.
- Record `lastVerified` whenever technical behavior is reviewed.
- Label vendor-specific examples and avoid implying endorsement.

## Structure

Lead with the reader’s task or question. Use descriptive headings, short examples, and a concise conclusion that points to a next action. Define terms at first use and link to the glossary rather than redefining them repeatedly.
